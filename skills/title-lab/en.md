# title-lab: English title types and platform rules

Researched from English sources (listed at the end), not translated from the Chinese book. English readers meet a title out of context (a feed, a search result, the HN front page), so it has to say what the piece is about before it tries to intrigue.

## Title types (use only when the material condition holds)

| Type | Material condition |
|---|---|
| Plain statement | One concrete finding or event the reader can grasp in one line ("Birds Heal PTSD" beats "Alternative Treatment for PTSD"). Default for HN. |
| How-to / benefit | The piece gives the reader a method they can use. Name the result, not the topic. |
| Pain point | The reader has a problem the piece names and answers ("Why X keeps failing"). |
| Proof | A real number or outcome from the material ("How one config flag cut our deploy time in half"). |
| Contrast | Expected vs actual, before vs after, or the author against the common view. |
| Question | The piece answers a question the reader already asks. The answer must be in the body's first third. |
| Curiosity gap | One odd, true detail shown half-way; the opening completes it. Risky: a gap the body does not close is clickbait. |
| Audience call-out | The reader is a group nameable in a few words ("For on-call engineers: ..."). |

## Platform rules

- **Hacker News:** use the piece's plain, accurate title. No uppercase for emphasis, no exclamation marks, no praise of the article, no gratuitous number ("10 Ways To Do X" becomes "Ways To Do X"), no editorializing or linkbait. Put plain statement and proof candidates first.
- **Blog / newsletter:** front-load the most specific word; aim for a title that still makes sense alone in a feed or search result. Prefer clear over clever; puns hide the keyword.
- **All:** write more candidates than you need (10+ variants is common practice) and pick; the promise must match what the piece delivers.

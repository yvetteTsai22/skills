## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Definition.** A Zettelkasten is a personal tool for thinking and writing: a hypertext of individually addressable notes that puts *connection, not collection* first. It amplifies effort and does not replace it. Expect a slow start, as when learning to swim.

**Three traits** to test any system: *hypertextual*, *atomic*, *personal* (one box per person, one person per box). **Anatomy of a Zettel**: unique ID, a body in your own words, references. A note without references is your own thought.

**Minimum viable system**: IDs as addresses plus links as hypertext. Everything else is optional. Use **time-based IDs** (`202006110955`) digitally. Luhmann-IDs (`1a1`) suit paper only, and titles-as-IDs break when you rename.

**Principles over techniques** ("principles produce techniques in an instant"). Copy Luhmann's principles (addressability, connection, sparse entry points, organic growth), not his paper-forced techniques. Before adopting a practice, ask whether paper forced it.

**Link context.** Every link should state why it exists, because the stated reason is created knowledge. Bare links erode trust in the web and make shallow work a habit. Ranked ties: adjacency < full-text match < tag < structure-note membership < annotated direct link. **Set links by hand.** Search alone fails because vocabulary drifts and skimming hits is tiring. Links preserve one-off associations.

**Structure notes over Folgezettel.** Folgezettel give a single hierarchy whose meaning is removed on purpose. Structure notes give *unlimited* hierarchies that are each meaningful locally: "order without limiting yourself to one order." One note can sit in *Self-Development* and in *Time*. Collecting connections to interpret later is the Collector's Fallacy for links.

**No categories; object tags.** Don't pre-design a taxonomy. Let clusters emerge. Tag what a note is *about* (object), not what it is *relevant to* (topic): use a sniper rifle, not a shotgun.

**The Collector's Fallacy.** Knowing *about* is not knowing. Copies, bookmarks, and read-later queues are self-rewarding alibis. Counter with short **research → read → assimilate** cycles (start with a 1 h cap) and budget up to 2× reading time for note processing.

**Building blocks and boundary.** Inbox → note archive → reference manager. Finished texts sit *outside* the system, so new insights from drafting become new Zettel.

**Structure emerges with scale.** Content notes come first, then hub and structure notes (when tag searches overflow, roughly 500–1,500 notes), then top-level structure notes (designed hubs marked `##tag`). Avoid folders and project silos, because they cap complexity and block interdisciplinary links.

**Layers of evidence.** Keep the *pattern* (with study conditions) apart from the *interpretation* (fragile) and the *synthesis*. Check primary literature.

**Barbell reading.** Pass 1: read swiftly and mark. Pass 2: process only the marks, deeply. Quality sets the time spent. **Reading is searching** for theories, models, definitions, arguments, counter-arguments, and facts. Workflow: dot the margin, write own-words slips, **cluster by purpose (not chapter)**, write a general note, then detail notes, then update the general note.

**Constructivist reading.** Information is created by your filter, not stored in texts. Keep your interpretations and give up "100% coverage".

**Communication partner.** Luhmann's conditions are irritation (surprise), information, and complexity (notes × relations). Grow both and let the archive surprise you. Learning runs *coverage → practice → insight*.

**Writing from notes.** Attach every new Zettel to an outline or structure note (indirectional work turned directional). Draft by assembly: survey, outline, attach notes, fill gaps, paste, rewrite. **Separate composing from revising.** Schedule writing as appointments and track notes and words per day without moralising the numbers. Publish iteratively.

**Getting started.** One genuine research interest, one structure note, three skills (create, link, find). Create notes link-first. **One Zettelkasten for life**, unless the project is truly disposable. The first note doesn't matter, but your intent (project box vs. knowledge companion) does. Split a note when getting information out takes too long: two details plus a connector, keeping the original ID.

# LinkedIn Post Writer

## Overview

Write high-performing LinkedIn posts using proven hook, body, and CTA structures. Match format to the idea. Write to one person, not an audience.

## Step 1: Get the Core Idea

Ask these questions (skip any the user already answered):
1. What's the one insight or takeaway?
2. Who is the target audience?
3. Any specific hook or angle?
4. What format? (Listicle, story, hot take, lesson learned, framework — or let me pick)
5. What industry shift is the reader already seeing that makes this matter? (e.g. "teams are building software factories — AI writes the code, another AI reviews it")

If the user can't answer question 5, don't invent the framing silently — find it in Step 1.5 and flag it as an assumption.

If the user provides raw notes or a retro, extract the insight directly and confirm before writing.

**Default reader when unknown:** a smart outsider — a PM, manager, or founder who follows the topic but doesn't do the hands-on work. Not a peer specialist. Specialists still read posts written for outsiders; outsiders drop posts written for specialists.

## Step 1.5: Set the Scene

Before writing, answer: what does this reader need to know before my story makes sense? A post that opens on the author's setup ("my 8 agents…") assumes the reader already shares the author's world.

Build the opening as a ladder, one or two lines per rung:
1. **The shift** — what's happening in the industry or the reader's world right now
2. **How people handle it** — the common practice or assumption
3. **My experience** — where that practice broke for me
4. **The insight** — what I learned

**Research the shift before writing rung 1.** Dispatch a small research subagent (web search, read-only, a few searches max) with the post's topic. Ask it to return:
- the 2–3 terms people in this space are using right now for the shift (e.g. "software factory", "agentic coding"), each with one source link and date from the last ~3 months
- one line on what each term means to a non-specialist
- which term a PM or manager would most likely recognise

Use the term the outsider reader would recognise, spelled out in plain words the first time it appears. Pick one term, not a pile of buzzwords. If the user gave a framing in question 5, the research only checks the wording is current; the user's framing wins. List the chosen term and its source in your assumptions.

Details (tool names, counts, internal categories) come after the insight, and only the ones the reader needs. Keep one or two numbers that carry the story; cut the rest.

## Step 1.75: Pick a Mode

Ask which mode the user wants (default **Insight** if they can't answer). Context rules (Step 1.5, Step 3.5, the Context checklist, banned sentence shapes) apply to every mode.

| Mode | Opens with | Shape | Length |
|------|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Insight** (default) | The result or the industry shift | Scene ladder → experience → insight → what I do now | 800–1,300 chars |
| **Zoom-in** | Something the reader already sees in their own world | Wide view → narrower → the problem → my case → what it means for you | 1,000–1,500 chars |

### Zoom-in mode rules
Walk the reader in from the general to the specific, like a camera slowly zooming. The reader should feel they arrived at the problem themselves before the author shows up.
- **Start wide, from the reader's world.** Open with a shift or habit the reader recognises ("Most teams now let AI write code. Fewer have asked who checks the AI."), not with the author.
- **Each paragraph narrows one level**: industry → how teams handle it → where that quietly breaks → my case as one example → back out to what it means for the reader.
- **Talk to the reader**: "you", "your team", "most of us". The author's experience is evidence, not the centre.
- **Connect paragraphs with plain logic words** (so, but, which means, and that's where). Each step should follow from the last; no jumps.
- **Author's details arrive late and small**: one or two concrete facts that prove the point.
- **Zoom back out at the end**: one line on what this means for the reader's team, then the CTA.
- **No cinematic openings.** Never "It was Friday afternoon.", "Picture this:", "Imagine…", "Let me tell you a story.", or invented time/place scene-setting. These read as AI.

## Step 2: Pick the Right Format

| Format | Best for | Optimises |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Listicle | Tips, lessons, frameworks | Saves |
| Story arc | Personal experiences, turning points | Comments |
| Hot take / contrarian | Challenging conventional wisdom | Shares |
| Before/after | Transformations, results | Engagement |
| Framework / mental model | Teaching a concept | Saves |
| Observation | Industry trends, things you've noticed | Speed |

## Step 3: Write the Post

### Hook (Line 1)
- ONE line only. Two-line hooks drop reach.
- Must work on mobile — only the first 2 lines show before "...see more."
- Determines 80% of the post's performance.

Hook formulas:
- **Bold claim**: "Most people fail on LinkedIn because they write for everyone."
- **Specific result**: "I went from 0 to 50K followers in 9 months. Here's the one thing that mattered."
- **Counterintuitive take**: "The best content strategy I ever used was posting less."
- **Curiosity gap**: "I've studied 500+ viral posts. The ones that blow up do one thing differently."
- **Relatable pain**: "You know that feeling when you spend an hour on a post and get 3 likes?"
- **Transformation hook**: "I packed a one-way ticket and it changed everything."

### Re-Hook (Line 2)
Adds heft, creates contrast, or preempts an objection. Locks the reader in.

Re-hook patterns:
- **Add heft / preempt objection**: "How to get your first 10 clients on LinkedIn" → "without spending a single dollar"
- **Create contrast**: "LinkedIn is the #1 business platform" → "But it's the worst platform to sell your products"
- **Parenthetical qualifier**: "How I grew my audience 10x faster:" → "(Without having to post 7x a week)"

### Body (Lines 3–20)
- Never write paragraphs longer than 3 lines.
- Alternate single lines with short clusters of 2–3 sentences (1-3-1 rhythm).
- Be specific: numbers, names, timeframes. "$127K in 12 months" beats "good revenue."
- Each point should be scannable — a reader skimming gets 70% of the value.
- Add one "signpost" sentence: show HOW you know what you're about to share. Turns generic advice into credible insight.

### CTA (Last 2 lines)
- One CTA only.
- Best CTAs invite conversation, not clicks. Ask a question that's easy to answer.
- P.S. format works well: "P.S. What's worked best for you?"

### The "Write to One Person" Method
Write every post to one specific person — a friend, a mentee, a past version of yourself. If it sounds like a coffee conversation, it works. If it sounds like a keynote speech, rewrite it.

## Formatting Rules
- Target 800–1,300 characters (Insight mode) or 1,000–1,500 (Zoom-in mode). Under 800 feels thin.
- One emoji max, only if it genuinely adds meaning. Zero is fine.
- No hashtags in the body. Add 3–5 at the very end after a line break if the user wants them.
- One sentence per line when possible. White space is your friend.
- Use `→` or `—` for visual breaks instead of bullet points in flowing text.
- Numbered lists use `1.` format, not emojis as numbers.

## Step 3.5: Cold-Read Pass

Before presenting, reread the draft as the outsider reader from Step 1 who has never seen the author's setup. Mark:
- the first line where they'd stop understanding
- every term they'd need to look up
- the first line where they'd scroll away

Fix each mark: add the missing context, swap jargon for plain words, or cut. If a subagent is available, have one do this read with only the draft and the reader description.

## Step 4: Offer Variations

After the first draft, always offer:
- "Want me to try a different hook?"
- "Want a shorter/longer version?"
- "Want me to shift the tone (more personal / more authoritative / more provocative)?"

## Pre-Publish Checklist

Run silently before presenting the draft. Fix issues first.

**Hook:**
- [ ] Hook works with ZERO context — read line 1 only, would you tap "see more"?
- [ ] Hook is ONE line only
- [ ] Re-hook (line 2) adds contrast, heft, or handles an objection

**Context:**
- [ ] First 3 lines make sense to the outsider reader with no prior knowledge of the author's setup
- [ ] Scene is set (shift → common practice) before the author's personal details
- [ ] At most 2 insider terms in the whole post, each explained in plain words
- [ ] No more than 3 numbers; each one carries the story

**Substance:**
- [ ] At least ONE specific number, name, or timeframe
- [ ] Personal angle — couldn't have been written by anyone else
- [ ] Teaches, challenges, or reveals something — not just the obvious
- [ ] Signpost present — shows WHY the author has credibility on this

**Format:**
- [ ] Every paragraph 1–3 lines max
- [ ] Total character count within the chosen mode's range (Insight 800–1,300, Zoom-in 1,000–1,500)
- [ ] Scans well in 5 seconds

**CTA:**
- [ ] Exactly ONE call to action
- [ ] Easy to answer — low friction

**Cringe:**
- [ ] Reads like a coffee chat, not a keynote
- [ ] No "I'm excited to," "I believe that," or "It's important to"
- [ ] If the user's product appears, insight comes BEFORE the product

## Anti-Patterns

- Never start with "I'm excited to announce" or "Thrilled to share"
- Never use: delve, landscape, synergy, leverage, robust, streamline, cutting-edge
- Never use AI-sounding sentence shapes, even if the hook formulas seem to invite them: "X isn't A. It's B.", "The fix isn't A. It's B.", "It's not about A — it's about B.", a colon reveal ("It's one habit:"), or cinematic scene-setting ("It was Friday afternoon.", "Picture this:"). Say the point directly instead. Count them; zero is the target. An existing hook in this shape gets rewritten, not kept.
- Don't stack credentials up front (tool lists, agent counts, internal category names) to prove rigour. One plain sentence of credibility is enough.
- Don't tag people for engagement farming
- Don't ask people to "like, comment, and share" — pick ONE
- Don't write a post that could have been written by anyone
- Don't lead with your product. Lead with the insight.
- Don't write engagement bait with no substance ("Agree?" posts)
- Don't put CTA or sales pitch in the post body — use a pinned first comment

## Core Rules

- Write like a person, not a brand. Conversational > corporate.
- Every post must teach something, share something real, or challenge something specific.
- Always lead with value, not self-promotion.
- The hook gets them in the room. The re-hook slams the door behind them. Every line after earns the next line.
- The post should be strong enough with zero hashtags and zero emoji.

## How the Feed Ranks Posts (2026 research)

LinkedIn rebuilt its feed in March 2026 and published how it works. Write for these signals:

- **Long dwell** and **contribution** (comments, shares, reactions) are the two actions the ranking model learns from. A post that holds the reader 60+ seconds and draws real comments travels; a skimmed post does not. This is why the body must earn each next line.
- **Out-of-network reach goes by topic.** The feed shows posts to people who do not follow the author, on topics they care about, even in different words. Keep one topic per post and name it in plain words early.
- **Demoted by name**: "Comment 'Yes' if you agree" style bait, video with unrelated text, recycled thought leadership, engagement pods, comment automation. The CTA question must be one a reader answers with their own experience.
- **Comments after posting count.** Plan to reply to early comments with a real answer; replies extend the conversation the ranking reads.
- **Links**: LinkedIn published no penalty figure. Practitioners report lower reach for posts with an outbound link, and say the first-comment workaround no longer helps. If the post needs a link, make the post stand on its own without it.

Numbers circulating as "the algorithm" (saves worth 5x a like, -60% for links, ideal posting hour) are third-party estimates, not LinkedIn figures; do not cite them in the post.

# repurpose-flywheel: English

Researched from English sources, borrow tier (see Sources). Not a translation of the Chinese method.

## The idea: create once, distribute many times

Ross Simmonds' rule: put the effort into one strong piece, then redistribute it across channels for months. NPR's COPE (create once, publish everywhere) adds the engineering view: keep the content separate from its display, so one source feeds many templates. For a writer, the article is the source and each platform is a template.

Simmonds' rule for each piece: "Treat each repurposed asset as a new piece of content. Keep the core idea and rewrite it to match the platform's format, tone, voice." Identical posts everywhere underperform.

## Platforms and formats

| Platform | Format | Where the hook is | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blog / Substack | the pillar | title + first paragraph | 900-2500 words |
| Newsletter | behind-the-scenes note to existing readers | subject line | 300-600 words |
| X / Threads / Bluesky | thread | first post, a crisp claim | 5-10 posts |
| LinkedIn | personal post or carousel | first two lines before "see more" | 150-300 words, or 6-10 slides |
| Hacker News / Reddit | submit the pillar, or a self-post in the subreddit | title, plain and specific | title only, or 200-500 words |
| YouTube Shorts / Reels / TikTok | short video script | first 3 seconds | 30-90 seconds |
| YouTube / podcast | long talk-through | first 30 seconds | 8-20 minutes |
| Talk / meetup / workshop | slides or outline | the opening question | 10-30 minutes |

## Order

Start where the author already has an audience, then move outward. Pieces that collect comments (thread, Reddit, LinkedIn) go before the newsletter, so the newsletter can answer what readers asked.

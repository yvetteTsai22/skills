# repurpose-flywheel

One article is the **pillar**. Plan the pieces cut from it so each one feeds the next: a piece on one platform earns trust that the next piece cashes in, and the later pieces point readers back to the pillar. This is a plan, not five rewrites: write the pieces only when asked.

## 1. Pick the language file

Detect the language of the article. Read only one file:

- Chinese: [`zh.md`](zh.md)
- English or anything else: [`en.md`](en.md)

It holds the platform list and format rules for that language. Write the plan in that language.

## 2. Mine the pillar

List the article's reusable **assets**: each story, number, opinion, rule or quotable line, one line each, quoted short. Done when every story and number in the article is on the list; an asset the article does not contain is never added.

## 3. Plan the pieces

Produce at least 5 pieces, on at least 4 different platforms or formats. For each piece:

- Rebuild it for the platform: its own hook in that platform's first line or first 3 seconds, its own length and shape (the language file gives them). A pasted copy of the pillar is not a piece.
- Use one asset or a small set; different pieces lead with different assets.
- Give it a **job** in the flywheel: reach (new readers), trust (proof, depth), conversation (comments, replies), conversion (subscribe, follow, buy), or return (sends readers back to the pillar or to the next piece).

## 4. Output

A table, one row per piece:

| # | Platform | Format and length | Hook (first line, written out) | Assets used | Job | Points to |

Then: the publishing order with a reason (which piece goes first and why), and one line on what the author would make **next** from the comments these pieces collect.

Done when: 5+ rows; 4+ distinct platforms or formats; every hook is written out in full; every row names assets from step 2 and a job; at least 3 different jobs appear.

## Writing the pillar in the same run

When the run also asks for the article itself, write the article first as asked, then append the plan under a heading such as "Repurpose plan". Keep the article whole; the plan never replaces it.

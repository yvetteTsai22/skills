# content-preflight: the three questions in English

Researched from English sources (listed at the end), not translated from the Chinese book. English writing advice frames the same three checks as durability, novelty and purpose.

## Q1 Reuse: will this still earn its keep later?

Write once, use many times: a post becomes reference others link to, notes you build on, material cut into a talk, thread or README (swyx's "learn in public" knowledge base; Simon Willison's TILs and project write-ups, which outlive the project itself).

- Pass: the material holds something durable: a method, a number, a gotcha, a decision with its cost, a question readers will keep searching for.
- Fail: a reaction to this week's news, or a summary of what others already wrote.
- Fix: turn the one-off into the reusable part (the steps, the numbers, the failure mode); name the search query the piece answers; or make it the first of a series.

## Q2 Sameness: is this the take everyone already wrote?

Useful writing tells people something "true and important that they didn't already know" (Paul Graham). Orwell's check on images applies to whole pieces: is it fresh enough to have an effect?

- Pass: the author has a position, number or experience the default take lacks, and the piece leads with it.
- Fail: same angle, same structure, same examples as the top results for the headline.
- Fix: lead with what only the author saw (the cost, the failure, the number); write from a party nobody writes from (hand off to `angle-finder`); cut the parts every other post covers.

## Q3 Real goal: what is the piece for?

Orwell's first question, "What am I trying to say?", plus the technical-writing habit of naming the audience and what they need to know or do afterwards (Google Technical Writing). Importance is "how many people it matters to times how much it matters to each" (Graham).

- Pass: one sentence names the reader, what they know or do after reading, and what the author wants back (feedback, trust, users, a record).
- Fail: the goal is "write something good about X", or the piece serves the author's wish to say it more than any reader's need.
- Fix: write "After reading, the reader will ___"; drop topic parts that do not serve that line; for HN or launch posts, name the specific feedback you want.

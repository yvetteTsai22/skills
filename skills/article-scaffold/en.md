# article-scaffold: English templates and selection rules

Researched from English sources (listed at the end), not translated from the Chinese book. English blog and HN readers skim and leave early, so the point arrives sooner than in a Chinese feature.

## Opening (pick one)

| Template | Material condition |
|---|---|
| Anecdotal lead + nut graf | A concrete moment (a scene, a spoken line, a number that surprised someone). Follow it within 2–3 paragraphs with the **nut graf**: one paragraph saying what the piece is about and why the reader should care. |
| Answer first | The reader arrives with a question (a how-to, a decision, a postmortem). State the conclusion in the first sentence or two; the rest supports it. |
| Situation, complication, question | The reader needs context before the answer lands: state the situation they recognise, what changed or broke, the question that raises, then the answer. |
| Most important sentence first | No better opening exists yet. Say the single most important thing plainly and move on. |

Rules: the first paragraph carries a concrete noun, number or quote from the inventory. Never open on throat-clearing ("In today's world...") or on a definition.

## Structure (pick one)

| Template | Material condition |
|---|---|
| Inverted pyramid | Reference, news, HN launch posts: rank items by how much the reader needs them and cut from the bottom. |
| Point + evidence groups | One claim with 2–4 supporting groups of evidence; each section opens with its own point and the groups never overlap. |
| Chronological arc | One experience with a before, a turn and an after (a decision and what it cost). Keep the timeline, but lift the peak into the opening and return to it. |
| Problem → solution | A problem the reader shares, then what was tried, what worked, what it cost. Good for HN posts and engineering write-ups. |

Decision order: platform is HN or the piece is a launch/tool post → inverted pyramid or problem → solution; one personal experience → chronological arc; one argued claim → point + evidence groups.

## Ending (pick one)

| Template | Material condition |
|---|---|
| Closing the circle | The opening has a place, person or line worth returning to. Default for personal essays. |
| Tie-back | An odd detail planted earlier can pay off at the end. |
| Epilogue | Something happened after the main story (a reversal, a cost discovered later). |
| Look to the future | The author has an honest open question or doubt about what comes next. |
| Apt quote | A line someone actually said sums up the piece. Overused; only with a real quote from the inventory. |

Rules: plant the ending early (the line or image you will return to should appear in the opening or the peak section). End on a concrete line, never a summary paragraph. Grab the ending when it appears; do not add a moral after it.

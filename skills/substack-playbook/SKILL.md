# substack-playbook

Turn the author's topic, draft or publication into something that works as a **Substack** newsletter: a **promise**, a post, a welcome email, or a growth plan. A Substack post is an email first: the subject line and the first screen decide whether the rest is read.

Work from the author's own material. Without it, mark the line `assumed`; never invent a subscriber count, open rate or reader quote.

## 1. Pick the branch

- **A Promise**: new publication, or the about page could fit any newsletter.
- **B Post**: a topic or draft to turn into an issue. Most requests land here.
- **C Welcome email**: setting up or rewriting what new subscribers get first.
- **D Growth**: the posts are fine, subscribers do not come.

Done when: branch named, with one sentence of reason.

## 2. A: the promise

1. Write the promise as `[reader] gets [specific result] through [method], every [rhythm]`. "Insights on productivity and growth" fails: a reader cannot check it.
2. Name the publication: the author's name plus the topic builds trust faster than a clever brand (Lenny's Newsletter).
3. Write the subtitle as `For [specific reader]: [result]`.
4. Rule the scope: the author writes only on what they know first-hand, and brings in an expert for the rest.

Done when: promise, 2-3 name options, subtitle, and an in/out scope list of 3 lines each.

## 3. B: the post

1. **Check against the promise.** The post must deliver something the promise names. If the publication has no promise yet, write a one-line guess and mark it `assumed`.
2. **Add something new.** Name the default take on the topic in one line, then state what this post adds to it: a number, a mistake, a method the reader can copy. A post that restates the default take is cut or reworked.
3. **Subject line**: about 8 words or fewer, so it is not cut off on a phone. Concrete: a number, a named thing, a result. The subject line is the post title unless the author wants them split.
4. **Preview text / subtitle**: one sentence that adds what the subject line leaves out; it shows beside the subject in the inbox.
5. **First screen** (about 100 words, what shows before scrolling on a phone): the situation and the stake. The reader knows why this matters to them before the background.
6. **Body**: actionable and concrete. Short paragraphs, plain subheads every 200-400 words, one idea per section. Every claim rests on the author's fact, number or story.
7. **Ending**: one ask. Best is a question readers can answer by replying to the email. Replies are the most direct signal a newsletter gets.
8. **Paywall** (only if the author has paid tiers): put the break after the post has already delivered one useful thing, so free readers see what they would pay for.

Done when: subject, preview, body and ending written; the post meets every line of the checklist in step 6.

## 4. C: the welcome email

The welcome email has the highest open rate of anything the author sends.

1. Subject line says plainly it is the welcome ("Welcome to [name]", "You're in. Start here").
2. One sentence: what the newsletter covers and how often.
3. Links to the 3 best past posts, each with a one-line reason to read it.
4. One question asking why the reader subscribed. Their replies, in their own words, are what to write next.

Done when: an email of about 150-250 words with all four parts.

## 5. D: growth

Pick 2-3 levers the author can run this month; give each one a weekly count.

- **Notes**: Substack's in-app short-form feed, not sent by email. Notes are the front door; many readers meet the author there first. Post short takes, quotes from posts (restack a quote), and comments on others' work. Use @mentions.
- **Recommendations**: recommend publications the author actually reads. The recommended writer is notified and prompted to recommend back.
- **Guest posts and cross-posts** in newsletters with the same readers.
- **Epic posts**: one deep, researched post now and then. Lenny reported that 50% of his early growth came from just two posts; the rest came from steady weekly issues.

Done when: a plan with 2-3 levers, a weekly count for each, and the metric to check (new free subscribers per week, replies).

## 6. Checklist for B

- [ ] Subject line ≤ about 8 words, concrete
- [ ] Preview text adds new information
- [ ] First screen states the stake for the reader
- [ ] The post adds something beyond the default take, named in step 3.2
- [ ] Every number and story comes from the author
- [ ] One ask at the end, answerable by reply

# medium-playbook

Turn the author's topic or draft into a **Medium** story that curators would Boost. Medium sorts every story into one of three tiers: **Boost** (curated, widest reach), **General Distribution** (shown to readers by interest and follows), and **Network Distribution** (followers only). The writing decides the tier; the steps below aim at Boost.

Work from the author's own material. Without it, mark the line `assumed`; never invent an experience, number or quote. Medium curates for human storytelling and demotes AI-generated writing, so the piece has to carry the author's own voice and facts.

## 1. Pick the branch

- **A Story**: a topic or draft to turn into a Medium story. Most requests land here.
- **B Placement**: the story exists; choose tags, a publication, and paywall or not.

Done when: branch named. A runs A then B.

## 2. A: the story

1. **Why this author.** Write one line: the first-hand experience that gives the author the right to write this. Boost guidelines ask for a clear reason this person wrote it. The first 3 paragraphs show that experience.
2. **Fresh, not derivative.** Name the default take on the topic in one line (what a search already returns). State what this story adds: a mistake, a number, a decision the reader can reuse. A story that restates easily found information gets General Distribution at best.
3. **Title**: specific beats clever. A concrete noun or number that the body pays off ("The Five Receipts Every Café Loses in December", not "10 Bookkeeping Tips"). A title that overpromises loses money twice: curators reject clickbait, and the read ratio drops when members open and leave.
4. **Subtitle**: names who the story is for and what they get. It shows under the title in feeds.
5. **Opening**: hook within the first screen; the stake and the author's experience before background.
6. **Body**: one idea per section, subheads a skimmer can follow, every claim on the author's facts. Source any outside fact with a link. Length serves the story: short is fine, padding is not.
7. **Ending**: what the reader can do or think differently tomorrow. Leave out pitches about earning on Medium, Boost or the Partner Program; stories about those go to Network Distribution.
8. **Images**: an original cover image when the author has one; alt text and a caption on each image; any AI-generated image labeled as such.

Done when: title, subtitle, story and ending written, and every line of the checklist in step 4 passes.

## 3. B: placement

1. **Tags**: up to 5, specific tags readers follow ("Small Business Accounting", not "Business").
2. **Publication**: submit to an edited publication whose stories match this one in topic and tone; editors improve the piece and its followers extend reach. List 2-3 candidates with a one-line fit reason, or mark `assumed` without web access.
3. **Paywall** (Partner Program): paywalled stories earn from members' reading time and engagement. Paywall only stories the author wrote; AI-generated writing cannot be paywalled.

Done when: tags, 2-3 publication candidates, and a paywall yes/no with a reason.

## 4. Checklist for A

- [ ] The first 3 paragraphs show the author's first-hand experience
- [ ] The story adds something beyond the default take, named in step 2.2
- [ ] Title is specific and the body pays it off
- [ ] Subtitle names the reader
- [ ] Every number and story comes from the author; outside facts are linked
- [ ] No content about Medium earnings, Boost, or the Partner Program
- [ ] Every image has alt text and a caption
